## Per-record findings

### `saraswati-aim` — supported (medium confidence)

The Devanagari `ॐ ऐं सरस्वत्यै नमः` and IAST `Oṃ Aiṃ Sarasvatyai Namaḥ`
are internally consistent (dative *Sarasvatyai*, anusvāra rendered `ṃ`). A
traditional Sarasvatī bīja/namaḥ attribution is appropriately modest; no
precise scripture should be implied. The brief meaning, deity, study/focus
intentions, morning/midday contexts, and `rotateOften` classification are
plausible and calm. The phrase “for clear thought” is an interpretive intended
use, not a guaranteed result. No strong textual correction identified.

### `lakshmi-shrim` — supported (medium confidence)

The text `ॐ श्रीं महालक्ष्म्यै नमः` and `Oṃ Śrīṃ Mahālakṣmyai Namaḥ` match
the cited traditional-form corroboration. “Lakṣmī bīja mantra (traditional)” is
honest; it should not be promoted to a Vedic citation without evidence. The
meaning is respectful, though “source of abundance and grace” remains a
theological framing for a human reviewer to accept or simplify. Deity,
gratitude/prosperity intent, daytime contexts, and `rotateOften` are plausible.
No strong textual correction identified.

### `devi-dum` — supported (medium confidence)

The displayed `ॐ दुं दुर्गायै नमः` and `Oṃ Duṃ Durgāyai Namaḥ` are a known
traditional form; the bīja is properly marked with `ṃ` in IAST. “Traditional”
is the right level of attribution. The protective/courage framing and listed
contexts are plausible and not outcome-guaranteeing. “Fierce and guarding
mother” is devotional interpretation, so a human reviewer may choose a more
literal “Salutations to Durgā” meaning, but the evidence does not establish a
required text change.

### `devi-navarna` — needs human judgment (medium confidence)

The Devanagari `ॐ ऐं ह्रीं क्लीं चामुण्डायै विच्चे` and IAST `Oṃ Aiṃ Hrīṃ
Klīṃ Cāmuṇḍāyai Vicce` are a recognized Navārṇa/Navākṣarī recitational form;
`vicce` is a defensible IAST rendering of `विच्चे`. However, bīja-mantra
practice and whether to present the form with a leading *Oṃ* vary by Śākta
lineage. The present source title overstates a direct textual locus: mark the
record for a qualified Śākta reviewer rather than treating “Devī Māhātmya” as a
precise verse citation. The meaning’s assignment of the three bījas to “will,
knowledge, and action” is lineage-sensitive and should not be treated as a
settled translation. Protection/courage/devotion, dawn/night, and
`occasional` are plausible but still require that same human judgment.

Suggested source wording *if the reviewer accepts this tradition-level
description*: `Traditional Śākta Navārṇa (Navākṣarī) mantra used in Caṇḍī/Devī
Māhātmya practice.`

### `surya-gayatri` — correction candidate (high confidence)

The Sanskrit and IAST accurately represent a common liturgical Gāyatrī form;
the plain meaning faithfully conveys the central RV verse. `surya`, dawn,
study/clarity/wisdom/devotion, and `dailyAnchor` are appropriate. The source
claim needs precision: RV 3.62.10 is the three-pāda *tat savitur ...
pracodayāt* verse; the leading *Oṃ bhūr bhuvaḥ svaḥ* is the familiar liturgical
praṇava/mahāvyāhṛti addition and should not be silently represented as the
complete Rigvedic verse.

Exact suggested `sourceTitle` correction:

`Gāyatrī mantra—Ṛgveda 3.62.10, in common liturgical form with praṇava and mahāvyāhṛtis`

The matching `provenance.sourceReference` would need the same distinction when
a human authorizes any canonical edit.

### `surya-namah` — supported (high confidence)

`ॐ सूर्याय नमः` / `Oṃ Sūryāya Namaḥ` is internally correct Sanskrit/IAST and
is accurately described as traditional. The brief translation, Sūrya deity,
dawn/leaving-home moments, clarity/courage intentions, dawn/morning contexts,
and `rotateOften` all fit the simple namaḥ invocation. Tone is respectful;
no strong correction identified.

### `shanti-triple` — correction candidate (high confidence)

`ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः` / `Oṃ Śāntiḥ Śāntiḥ Śāntiḥ` is correct. The
record is better described as a traditional triple peace invocation than as a
single identifiable “Upaniṣadic” mantra, because it occurs as the closing of
many different śānti mantras. “For body, mind, and spirit” is a particular
popular interpretation, not the neutral literal sense. Universal deity,
peace intent, evening/night contexts, and `dailyAnchor` are suitable.

Exact suggested corrections:

- `sourceTitle`: `Traditional triple Śānti invocation`
- `meaning`: `A threefold invocation of peace.`

### `shanti-asato-ma` — supported (high confidence)

The Sanskrit, IAST, and translation correspond to the Pavamāna passage at
Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 1.3.28. The source attribution is precise, though the
app presents the three lines as a prayer excerpt rather than its surrounding
prose/ritual context. Universal deity, clarity/peace/wisdom intentions,
dawn/night contexts, and `rotateOften` are respectful product classifications.
No strong correction identified.

### `shanti-sarve-bhavantu` — needs human judgment (medium confidence)

The Devanagari, IAST (including `duḥkhabhāg bhavet`), and concise translation
are well supported by common recitational compilations. The universal tone and
peace/gratitude contexts are appropriate. The assertion “traditional
Upaniṣadic invocation” lacks a verified precise primary location in this
review; do not upgrade or approve it on that attribution. A qualified reviewer
should either document a reliable textual source/variant or retain only an
honest tradition-level description. Morning/sunset and `rotateOften` are
reasonable product classifications, not text-derived prescriptions.

### `shanti-lokah-samastah` — correction candidate (high confidence)

`लोकाः समस्ताः सुखिनो भवन्तु` / `Lokāḥ samastāḥ sukhino bhavantu` is
consistent and is documented in a longer traditional closing invocation. The
existing traditional source title is appropriately cautious, but the English
adds “and free,” which the displayed Sanskrit does not say. The universal,
goodwill-oriented classification and sunset/night `dailyAnchor` are respectful
and plausible.

Exact suggested `meaning` correction:

`May all worlds be happy.`

### `shanti-saha-navavatu` — correction candidate (high confidence)

The displayed core Sanskrit/IAST is correct, and its cooperative study meaning
is faithful. The source tradition is sound, although it occurs across multiple
Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda/Upaniṣadic settings; a human reviewer should decide whether the
combined Taittirīya/Kaṭha label meets the project’s precision rule. Standard
attested recitations include the closing `ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः`, omitted
from this record. Study/meeting/before-work, clarity/focus/peace, daytime, and
`rotateOften` are appropriate.

Exact suggested additions, if the intended catalog form is the complete śānti
mantra:

- append primary text: `\nॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः॥`
- append transliteration: `\nOṃ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ.`
